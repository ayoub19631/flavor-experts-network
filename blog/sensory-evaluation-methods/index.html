Sensory Evaluation Methods for Flavor Professionals

An overview of discrimination, descriptive, and affective testing methods used to validate flavor prototypes and ensure product quality.

Reliable sensory evaluation is the bridge between analytical chemistry and consumer acceptance. Flavor professionals use structured panels to quantify differences that instruments alone cannot capture.

Common Test Methods

Discrimination Testing

Triangle and duo-trio tests determine whether samples are perceptibly different—essential when reformulating for cost or supply constraints.

Descriptive Analysis

Trained panels generate quantitative profiles using standardized lexicons, enabling comparison across prototypes and competitors.

Affective Testing

Consumer hedonic scales measure liking and purchase intent before launch.

Best Practices

  • Control serving temperature and portion size
  • Use blind coding and randomized presentation order
  • Document panelist training and calibration sessions

Integrating sensory data with GC-MS and electronic nose outputs creates a fuller picture of flavor performance throughout product development.
